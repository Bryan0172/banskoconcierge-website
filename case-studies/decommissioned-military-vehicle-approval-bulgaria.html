Case Study: Registering a Decommissioned Military Vehicle in Bulgaria

AD
Andreas Donner
· 9 October 2026 · Case Study

A verbal “no” at an inspection counter is not a rule. It is the beginning of a question.

Owners who bring a decommissioned military vehicle to Bulgaria, usually a truck rebuilt as an expedition camper, tend to hear the same answer: not possible, “because of a certain rule”. Nobody names it.

So I read the primary texts, and then put the question to the approval authority in writing.

The written answer turned the situation round. Vehicles built for the armed forces are exempt from type-approval, but in the authority’s reading only while they are in service. Once a vehicle is decommissioned and converted for civilian use, an approval procedure is not forbidden. It is required. For a vehicle never registered anywhere, both the EU and the national individual approval are open, on the owner’s application. For decommissioned military vehicles, the authority has confirmed in writing that it has already issued approval certificates.

That is a procedure, not a promise: the technical inspection still decides the outcome.

What it changes is the conversation. Instead of asking the next inspector what they remember, you know which provision applies, and you have it on paper.

About this case

Published without the owner’s name, company or vehicle data. The same approach applies to other vehicles that are hard to register in Bulgaria: we read the provision, ask the authority in writing and work from the written answer. See also Car import and registration in Bulgaria.

This case study is for general information only and does not constitute legal advice. The outcome of any individual approval depends on the technical inspection of the specific vehicle.
