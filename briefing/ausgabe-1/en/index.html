Prices up, sales down: Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas, the property transfer tax and what to check before the notary

In Bulgaria's four major cities, far fewer purchase deeds were registered from January to September 2026 than a year earlier. Even so, apartment prices in the second quarter were 9 to 14% above the previous year. This issue sets out the figures for Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna and Burgas with their source and date, explains what the municipality charges in tax on a purchase, and lists five points that should be settled before the notary appointment.

Market picture: Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas

Anyone buying in Bulgaria today is buying in a market where sellers expect higher prices but fewer buyers sign. This is not true of one region only but of all four major cities: Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna and Burgas. Three figures show it.

1. Sales: far fewer purchases in all four major cities

From January to September 2026, Bulgaria registered 18.8% fewer purchase deeds than in the same period a year earlier. Every major city is down:

3. Building: new homes mainly in Sofia and Burgas

In Q2 2026, permits were issued for 11,499 dwellings in residential buildings across Bulgaria, 14.0% more than a year earlier. In the four cities:

  • Sofia (capital): 2,878 (+13.7%)
  • Plovdiv: 1,941 (−2.7%)
  • Burgas: 1,660 (+34.2%)
  • Varna: 1,316 (−1.1%)

Quarterly figures at district level fluctuate strongly. These numbers describe the second quarter, not a trend.

Source: НСИ (National Statistical Institute), building permits and construction starts, Q2 2026 and Q2 2025.

On currency: Since 1 January 2026 the euro has been Bulgaria's currency, at the fixed rate of 1.95583 leva. Purchase prices, tax values and notary fees are stated in euro (Regulation (EU) 2025/1409).

Law and tax: the transfer tax on a property purchase

Anyone buying property in Bulgaria pays a transfer tax to the municipality. Each municipality sets the rate itself, and the tax base does not always start from the purchase price.

  • Who pays: the buyer. In force (Local Taxes and Fees Act, ЗМДТ, Art. 45(1)).
  • Tax base: the agreed price, but at least the tax assessment value (данъчна оценка) of the property. Stated in euro since 2026. In force (ЗМДТ Art. 46).
  • Range: the municipal council sets the rate between 0.1% and 3%. In force (ЗМДТ Art. 47(2)).
  • When due: on transfer, in practice before the notary appointment, payable to the municipality where the property is located. In force (ЗМДТ Art. 49).
  • Sofia, Plovdiv, Varna, Burgas: 3% each, the statutory maximum. In force (municipal tax ordinances: Столична община Art. 35(2), Община Пловдив Art. 35(2), Община Варна Art. 34(2), Община Бургас Art. 41(2)).
  • From 2027: the Ministry of Finance intends to raise the base values used for tax assessment. That raises the floor of the tax base. Draft. The public consultation (No. 12735) closed on 23.10.2026. Nothing has been adopted yet.

What this means before the notary: all four major cities apply the maximum rate of 3%. It is charged on the higher of the purchase price and the tax assessment value. It therefore pays to have the current tax assessment value of the property in writing before you sign.

What should be checked before the notary, besides the money

Before a purchase, the law firm checks ownership, encumbrances and the contract. It does not check the condition of the building. These five points therefore belong before the preliminary contract, at the latest before the notary appointment:

  1. Structure: Are there visible cracks or signs of settlement? What condition are the roof, façade and balconies in, as far as they are accessible?
  2. Damp and mould: Is there visible damp or mould, condensation marks or any sign of earlier water damage?
  3. Building services: Inspect heating, hot water and ventilation visually. Does any installation have obvious defects?
  4. Documents: Do the papers provided show Act 16 and the occupancy permit? If not, what stage has construction reached? The legal assessment belongs with the law firm.
  5. Common parts: Look at the staircase, basement, roof and plant rooms where they affect the value or the running costs of the apartment.

Why before: before the notary appointment, a finding can still be negotiated. After it, it can only be repaired.
